📖 Acid–Base Theory

🔴

What is an Acid?

An acid is a substance that donates hydrogen ions (H⁺) in solution. Acids have a pH less than 7, taste sour, and turn blue litmus paper red. Common examples include HCl (Hydrochloric acid), H₂SO₄ (Sulfuric acid), and CH₃COOH (Acetic acid).

🔵

What is a Base?

A base is a substance that accepts hydrogen ions (H⁺) or donates hydroxide ions (OH⁻). Bases have a pH greater than 7, taste bitter, and turn red litmus paper blue. Common examples include NaOH (Sodium hydroxide) and NH₃ (Ammonia).

⚗️

Neutralization Reaction

When an acid reacts with a base, a neutralization reaction occurs. The products are typically a salt and water. The general equation is:

Acid + Base → Salt + Water

The pH of the final solution depends on the relative strengths of the acid and base.

🌡️

pH Scale

pH measures how acidic or basic a solution is, ranging from 0–14. pH 7 is neutral (pure water). Below 7 is acidic; above 7 is basic/alkaline. Each unit change represents a 10× change in H⁺ concentration.

Acidic (0–6) Neutral (7) Basic (8–14)
💡

Arrhenius Theory

Proposed by Svante Arrhenius (1884). An acid produces H⁺ ions in water; a base produces OH⁻ ions. This is the simplest theory but only applies to aqueous solutions.

🔬

Brønsted–Lowry Theory

A more general theory: an acid is a proton (H⁺) donor; a base is a proton acceptor. This explains acid-base reactions in non-aqueous solvents and includes substances like ammonia (NH₃) as a base.
